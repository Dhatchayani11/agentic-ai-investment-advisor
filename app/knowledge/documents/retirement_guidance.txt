RETIREMENT CONTRIBUTION GUIDANCE

Document type:
General educational retirement guidance

Source:
Internal prototype knowledge base grounded with current IRS guidance.

Authoritative source:
Internal Revenue Service (IRS)
https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-401k-and-profit-sharing-plan-contribution-limits

Source verification date:
2026

Purpose:
Provide general educational information about retirement contributions.
This document is not personalized financial advice.

Key considerations:

1. Retirement contribution decisions should consider the customer's
financial circumstances, retirement goals, liquidity needs, and applicable
account rules.

2. Employer-sponsored retirement plans may have employer contribution
or matching arrangements. Customers should review their specific plan
documentation to understand applicable terms.

3. Retirement accounts have contribution limits and tax rules that may
change over time. Current limits must be verified against authoritative
IRS sources before being presented as current facts.

4. For 2026, the IRS states that the employee elective deferral limit
for traditional and safe-harbor 401(k) plans is $24,500.

5. For 2026, eligible participants age 50 or older may generally make
an additional 401(k) catch-up contribution of up to $8,000, subject to
applicable plan rules.

6. For 2026, a higher catch-up contribution limit of $11,250 applies to
participants who are age 60, 61, 62, or 63 in applicable plans, subject
to applicable rules.

7. Increasing retirement contributions can reduce current disposable
income while potentially increasing long-term retirement savings.

8. Investment values can rise or fall. Past performance does not
guarantee future results.

9. Personalized investment guidance should not be generated when
material customer information required for the assessment is unavailable.

10. Regulatory, tax, contribution-limit, and account-specific information
must be verified against current authoritative sources before being
presented as current facts.

Compliance principle:

The AI system must distinguish general educational information from
personalized investment recommendations and must not guarantee
investment returns or outcomes.

Regulatory source principle:

When a response contains current regulatory figures or limits, the
information must be traceable to an authoritative and current source.
If the system cannot establish adequate supporting knowledge, the
response must be routed for additional review.
